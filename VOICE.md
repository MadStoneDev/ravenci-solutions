# RAVENCI Voice Guide (for rewriting site copy)

Read this before touching any page copy on ravenci.solutions. The homepage (`/`) is the benchmark. It's the only page Richard has rewritten himself, and every other page should sound like the same person wrote it.

## Who's talking

Richard Haddad. One person, not an agency. He's been coding since he was 11 and building websites for about 25 years. It was a hobby for most of that, and it's been his business since 2018 (RAVENCI). Before that: construction sites, steel detailing and structural drafting, and running the office IT where he drafted.

He loves this work. He'd be doing it anyway; he's glad it's also his job. He can look at a WordPress site and tell you what's wrong with it. That confidence comes from years of doing it, and the copy should carry it quietly. Confident, not boastful.

He has no patience for marketing fluff, and neither do the people he wants as clients.

## How the homepage sounds

Study these lines. They're the target:

- "That's fine, because that's my job. I'm in it for the long run."
- "I host it, manage it, and throw in a free SSL (the padlock next to your domain)."
- "working daily backups, because backups that don't restore are useless."
- "SEO/AEO/GEO. Basically, when people search Google or ask AI, your business comes up."
- "Need something changed? Email me."
- "I design it, I build it, I host it, I patch it, and I answer the phone when something breaks."
- "The sites I built five years ago are still fast, still ranking, still running. That's the whole pitch."

What they have in common:

1. **First person, singular.** "I", never "we" or "our team". He does the work, so he says so.
2. **Plain words, jargon explained in passing.** When a technical term is needed, explain it in a few words in brackets or the next sentence. Write for a busy business owner, not a developer.
3. **A blunt verdict.** One short line that states the truth and moves on ("backups that don't restore are useless"). No hedging.
4. **Show, don't claim.** Specific facts beat adjectives: a PageSpeed score, a time frame, a real client, a real number. If you can't back a claim with a fact, cut it.
5. **Short sentences, casual asides.** Contractions, the occasional "Anything!" or "Email me." Australian English spelling (optimise, colour, licence).
6. **Calm confidence.** He knows he's good. He doesn't need to say it. Let the facts make the case.

## Never write

- Self-praise: "We're passionate about…", "industry-leading", "best-in-class", "world-class", "cutting-edge", "we can do anything", "your trusted partner", "we pride ourselves".
- Filler promises: "No surprises", "Results by design, not luck", "Launch day is just the beginning", "seamless", "elevate", "unlock", "empower", "take your business to the next level".
- Template tics already on the site: "not bolted on", "Built for how X actually runs", "The problems I hear, every time", stacked "X, not Y" contrasts.
- Benefit-only bullets with no fact behind them ("Real-time data", "Grows with you").
- Title Case Headings. Use sentence case.
- Cheesy lines ("Protected from the bad guys", "go viral").
- Em dashes (—). Use a comma, colon, full stop or brackets instead.

## How to work

1. Work page by page, staying one page ahead of Richard:
   - Finish Page 1, then **stop and wait**.
   - When Richard says he's started reviewing Page 1 (or Page N), draft Page 2 (or N+1). Never more than one page ahead of his review.
   - Apply his edits to a reviewed page before moving past it.
   - Keep each page's existing structure, components, section numbering and URLs unless asked otherwise.
2. Rewrite headings, intros, cards and FAQs to match this voice. Cut anything that fails the "would Richard say this out loud to a client?" test.
3. Make copy shorter, not longer. If a section says nothing specific, flag it for removal rather than padding it.
4. Keep every price, time frame and promise consistent with the facts above and with the other pages.
5. After each page, list for Richard:
   - what you changed,
   - any claims you weren't sure about (with `[TODO]` markers),
   - any section you'd suggest cutting.
6. Treat your output as a draft for Richard to edit, not final copy. He'll put the last pass in his own words.
